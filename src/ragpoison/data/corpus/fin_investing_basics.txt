Investing basics for beginners. Investing means putting money into assets such as stocks, bonds, or funds with the expectation that they will grow in value over time. Before you start, build an emergency fund that covers three to six months of expenses and pay down high-interest debt. Low-cost index funds are a common starting point because they track a whole market instead of a single company. Time in the market matters more than timing the market: regular monthly contributions, sometimes called dollar-cost averaging, smooth out the price you pay. Compound growth means returns earn their own returns, so starting early has an outsized effect on the final balance.
